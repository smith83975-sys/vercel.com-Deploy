Paper to metal: M2 and M1 backing calculators

This page asks a simple "what if" question: if every dollar in the US money supply had to be backed by metal, what price per ounce would that imply? Using M2 of $23.34 trillion (2026-08, FRED) and the US gold reserve of 261,498,926 troy ounces (US Treasury, as of 2026-08-31), full backing works out to about $89,265 per ounce of gold. Using all COMEX vault silver (333.20M oz, 2026-10-13), it's about $70,056 per ounce of silver. These are illustrations, not price targets.

The What backs a dollar? calculator widens the question to silver, gold, oil, natural gas, coal, copper and land. With every resource ticked by default, the total is about $31.89 trillion, or 136.6% of M2. Counting only what the federal government owns (gold reserve, Mint silver, Strategic Petroleum Reserve oil and federal land), it is about $2.92 trillion, or 12.5% of M2. Hypothetical: resources in the ground are not liquid and many are privately owned.

Spot vs M1 money calculator

How we calculate

Implied gold = backing% × M1 ÷ US gold reserve oz. Implied silver = backing% × M1 ÷ total COMEX silver oz (registered + eligible). Multiple = implied ÷ live spot.

Stored example (2026-10-15): at 30% backing, M1 $19.99 trillion (2026-08, FRED M1SL) gives about $22,934/oz gold and $17,999/oz silver.

What M1 and M2 are

M1 is the narrowest common measure of money: currency in circulation plus checking accounts and other balances you can spend immediately. M2 adds savings deposits, small time deposits and retail money market funds. Both are published monthly by the Federal Reserve and available on FRED (series M1SL and M2SL).

How to read the calculators

The "paper ratio" is the implied price divided by today's spot price. A paper ratio of 20 means there are about 20 times as many dollars in M2 as the dollar value of the gold reserve at today's price. The backing tiers (30%, 40%, 50%) show what partial backing would imply, since historical gold standards rarely backed every dollar.

There's an important difference between the two metals. The US government holds an official gold reserve, but it holds no official silver reserve. So for silver we compare the money supply with all silver in COMEX vaults (registered plus eligible). That's a reference pile of metal, not something the government owns.

Limits of this comparison

The US left the gold standard decades ago, and nothing suggests the dollar will be re-backed by metal. Money supply changes monthly, reserves are reported with a lag, and spot prices move constantly, so every figure here carries its own date. The calculation is a way to put the size of the money supply in perspective. It isn't a valuation model and shouldn't be read as a forecast.

FAQ

What is the implied gold price from M2?

US M2 money supply divided by US government gold reserve ounces. It is a hypothetical full-backing figure, not a price prediction.

Why does the silver calculation use COMEX vault silver?

The US holds no official silver reserve, so total COMEX silver (registered plus eligible) is used as a reference stock.

Could US resources like oil and land back the dollar?

Only as a thought experiment. The What backs a dollar? calculator compares M1 and M2 with dated estimates of US gold, silver, oil, gas, coal, copper and land. Most of those resources are in the ground, illiquid and privately owned, so this is an illustration, not a valuation or a prediction.
