Prices, conversions & what we left out
  • Money supply & debt: M2 $23.34T (2026-08, FRED M2SL); M1 $19.99T (2026-08, FRED M1SL); total public debt outstanding $40.41T (as of 2026-10-21, US Treasury, Debt to the Penny (FiscalData); $32.57T held by the public + $7.85T intragovernmental).
  • Silver spot: $54.08 /troy oz (as of 2026-10-23), gold-api.com (stored; live in browser).
  • Gold spot: $4,026.40 /troy oz (as of 2026-10-23), gold-api.com (stored; live in browser).
  • WTI crude spot (Cushing): $107.89 /barrel (as of 2026-10-20), FRED DCOILWTICO (EIA data).
  • Henry Hub natural gas spot: $3.10 /MMBtu (as of 2026-10-20), FRED DHHNGSP (EIA data).
  • Global copper price (monthly average): $14,473.06 /metric ton (as of 2026-09-01), FRED PCOPPUSDM (IMF data).
  • Average US coal sales price, all ranks (2024 annual): $52.47 /short ton (as of 2024), EIA Annual Coal Report 2024, Table 31.
  • Conversions: 1 metric ton = 32,150.7 troy oz (USGS); 1 Mcf natural gas = 1.04 MMBtu (EIA).
  • Federal land context: 615.2M acres managed by five federal agencies, about 640M acres in all (as of 2024-09-30, CRS R42346, Federal Land Ownership: Overview and Data (updated Sept 14, 2026)). BLM also administers 700M+ acres of federal subsurface mineral estate, so part of the oil, gas and coal reserves is federally owned, but EIA does not split its totals by owner.
  • Left out: Timber. No credible current national dollar valuation found. Larson (BEA) land values already include timber that conveys with the land, so adding it would double count.
  • Left out: Natural gas plant liquids. EIA reports 34,072 million barrels in proved reserves, but there is no single public NGL price series to value them on, so they are not counted.
  • Left out: Critical minerals other than copper (lithium, rare earths, etc.). USGS reserve figures exist for some, but there is no reliable public spot price for most, so they are left out rather than guessed.
  • Left out: Federal buildings, roads and other property. Not natural resources; outside this comparison.

    Physical tightness score

    How the score is calculated

    Score (0–100, higher = tighter) = average of two components:

    1. Inventory component = 100 − percentile of today's registered ounces within the recent daily history we hold (currently ~30 days for silver).
    2. Coverage component = 100 − min(100, total vault oz (registered + eligible) ÷ (futures open interest × contract size) × 100). Registered-only coverage is shown as a secondary line.

    If a component's input is unavailable it is shown as pending and the score uses the remaining component only (marked "partial"). The short history window means the percentile is a recent-trend measure, not a long-term one.

    What is force majeure?

    Force majeure is a contract clause that lets a party delay or skip its obligations when extraordinary events outside its control happen, such as war, a natural disaster, or a government order. In a COMEX context, it could allow delays to physical delivery during a genuine emergency instead of counting as a breach. This is general information only. It is not a prediction that it will happen, and it is not financial advice. Force majeure explained →
