Registered vs eligible, in plain English

Every business day, CME Group publishes how much metal sits in each COMEX-approved depository, such as Brink's, JPMorgan, HSBC and Loomis. Each depository's silver is split into two buckets.

Registered silver has a warehouse warrant attached. A warrant is the paperwork that makes a bar deliverable against a COMEX futures contract, so registered metal is what can be handed over when someone holding a contract asks for delivery. A standard COMEX silver contract (SI) is 5,000 troy ounces, usually met with roughly 1,000-ounce bars.

Eligible silver meets COMEX's bar and purity standards and sits in the same vaults, but has no warrant. It belongs to someone who is simply storing it. The owner can turn it into registered metal by having a warrant issued, and registered metal can be moved back to eligible. Silver can also leave the system entirely when an owner withdraws it.

Total vault silver

Total is registered plus eligible: every ounce of silver sitting in COMEX-approved vaults on the report date. On 2026-10-16 that was 334.87M oz. Total is the widest measure of the metal physically in the system. It moves only when silver is deposited into or withdrawn from the vaults, while shifts between registered and eligible are paperwork inside the same buildings. Over the 30 days to 2026-10-16, registered silver went from 96.45M oz (2026-09-14) to 103.12M oz, a change of +6.9% (+6.9% = 103.12M oz registered (Oct 16, 2026) ÷ 96.45M oz (Sep 14, 2026) − 1 (daily registered history in latest.json, CME Group warehouse report via The Vault Report).). Watching total and registered together tells you whether a move is metal arriving or leaving, or just being reclassified.

Paper-to-silver ratio (vs total vault silver)

The paper-to-silver ratio compares open futures contracts with metal in the vaults. Silver open interest was 104,389 contracts, about 521.95M oz (CME Group Daily Bulletin Section 02B; open interest as of CME bulletin fetched Oct 14, 2026, trade date not shown in extract). Measured against total vault silver (334.87M oz, 2026-10-16), that is about 1.56 ounces of paper per ounce in the vaults. Measured against registered silver only, it is about 5.06 to 1.

Headline ratio: 1.56 to 1 = 521.9M oz COMEX silver futures open interest (104,389 contracts × 5,000 oz; CME Daily Bulletin Sec. 02B, fetched Oct 14, 2026, trade date not shown) ÷ 334.9M oz total COMEX silver stocks, registered + eligible (CME Group warehouse report via The Vault Report, Oct 16, 2026).

Secondary ratio: 5.06 to 1 = 521.9M oz open interest (same as above) ÷ 103.12M oz registered COMEX silver (CME Group warehouse report via The Vault Report, Oct 16, 2026).

We lead with the total figure because eligible silver can be registered with paperwork, so it is the broadest reference. Neither number means contracts "can't be delivered": most futures are closed or rolled before expiry and never ask for metal. The ratio is context about the market's structure, not a forecast.

Delivery notices: issues and stops

During a delivery month, a short who wants to deliver files a notice. CME's daily delivery report shows issues (contracts where a clearing firm delivered metal) and stops (contracts where a firm received it), split into house and customer accounts. Each SI contract that is issued and stopped moves 5,000 ounces of registered silver from one owner to another. The metal usually stays in the vault; only the warrant changes hands. Month-to-date October silver deliveries were 3,511 contracts as of 2026-10-19. See the silver squeeze tracker and the deliveries tracker for daily entries.

How to read the CME report

  1. Find the date. The warehouse stocks report ("Silver Stocks") is dated with the activity date; it is published the next business day.
  2. Read by depository. Each vault has a row for registered and one for eligible, with the previous total, receipts (deposits), withdrawals, net change and adjustments.
  3. Check adjustments. A transfer between registered and eligible shows up as an adjustment, not a receipt or withdrawal.
  4. Sum the totals. The bottom lines give combined registered, eligible and total ounces, which is what we show here.
  5. Pair with deliveries. The separate delivery notices report shows issues and stops by firm. Compare it with registered stock to see how much of the deliverable cushion is being used.

Cautions

Moves between registered and eligible are often paperwork, not metal arriving or leaving. A falling registered number doesn't by itself mean a shortage, and a rising one doesn't mean a glut. Aggregator sites round eligible and total figures to 0.01M oz, and CME can revise reports. CME blocks automated downloads of its spreadsheets, so we take the figures from sites that republish them (The Vault Report, MetalCharts, Heavy Metal Stats) and cross-check where we can. A weekday routine refreshes latest.json and tank.json.

FAQ

What is the difference between registered and eligible silver?

Registered silver carries a warehouse warrant and can be delivered against a COMEX futures contract. Eligible silver meets COMEX standards and sits in the same vaults but has no warrant, so it is not deliverable until its owner registers it.

How much silver is in COMEX vaults?

As of 2026-10-16, 334.87M oz in total: 103.12M oz registered and 231.75M oz eligible (CME Group data via The Vault Report).

What is the COMEX paper-to-silver ratio?

Open interest in ounces divided by vault silver. With 521.95M oz of open interest and 334.87M oz total vault silver, it was about 1.56 to 1 (vaults 2026-10-16; open interest as of CME bulletin fetched Oct 14, 2026, trade date not shown in extract); against registered only, about 5.06 to 1.

What are issues and stops?

On CME delivery reports, issues are contracts where a firm delivered metal and stops are contracts where a firm received it.

How often is COMEX silver inventory updated?

CME Group publishes warehouse stocks each business day. VaultPulse refreshes its figures on weekdays and shows the report date next to every number.

How big is a COMEX silver contract?

The standard COMEX silver futures contract (SI) is 5,000 troy ounces.
