Where the headlines come from

Our server collects headlines from Google News search results for gold and silver, plus the Investing.com and FXStreet commodity feeds. It removes duplicates, sorts the newest first and caches the list for a few minutes. Each headline links to the original article and shows its source and how long ago it was published. Some feeds publish their times inconsistently, so a story can look slightly newer than it is.

What the alert icons mean

The 🚨 icon appears when a headline contains words that usually describe a major event, such as "crash," "plunge," "record high" or "force majeure." The ⚠️ icon marks notable moves or scheduled market events, such as a price slip, Federal Reserve decisions or inflation (CPI) reports. These icons come from simple keyword matching. They aren't our judgment of how important a story is, and they're not signals to buy or sell.

Reading market news carefully

Headlines are written to be clicked, and many of them include forecasts or price targets from banks and analysts. We pass along the headline as published, but we don't endorse any forecast. For context on a big headline, compare it with the dated data elsewhere on VaultPulse: COMEX inventory, deliveries, premiums and the gold-to-silver ratio.
